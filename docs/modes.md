title: Modes
---

Software in testing. STD is a 64-bit Windows application, Qt 6.11.1 MSVC. The rows below are what the Mode menu actually does in this tree. A mode is listed as keying only when TX builds its audio. On-air proof is still in progress.

Connect sends `AUDIO_START:0`. The text command START is never sent. CW stays in Lyra.

## Keying

| Menu | Rows | On the air path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FT8, FT4, FT2 | one each | MSHV generator. 15 s, 7.5 s, 3.75 s. DIGU. Decode rows sit under the Receive column titles. The Tx line marker is a biohazard sign, glowing green on the line that goes out next. Multi works the queue and rebuilds the next pass after each one. DIST is miles or kilometers from Options → Decode List Options. |
| Hell | Feld Hell, Slow Hell, Feld Hell X5, Feld Hell X9, FSK Hell, FSK Hell-105, Hell 80 | Fldigi column rates. Feld Hell's raised-cosine edge is then limited to the 245 Hz channel. The marker is centered on the carrier. Reverse video flips the tape. The tape is a draggable pane over the RX log. CLR RX clears the tape; CLR SENT clears the sent text. [Hell notes](hell.html). |
| RTTY | one row, labeled RTTY | The transmit pane has Shift, Baud, Shape, Reverse, and ITA-2. Shape is on, Reverse is off, and ITA-2 is off. 45.45 baud and 170 Hz are selected. The lists are Fldigi's: baud 45, 45.45, 50, 56, 75, 100, 110, 150, 200, 300, and shift 23, 85, 160, 170, 182, 200, 240, 350, 425, 850. Fldigi lists 45 baud first; 45.45 is the usual amateur speed. Five bits, 1.5 stop, mark high unless Reverse is on. US TTY figures unless ITA-2 is on. USOS: a space sends LTRS, then the space. Squelch gates the print on the signal-to-noise meter. AFC, off until you turn it on, moves the waterfall cursor. |
| PSK | PSK31, PSK63, PSK125, PSK250, PSK500, PSK1000 | BPSK. Symbol rates 31.25, 62.5, 125, 250, 500, 1000 baud. A zero reverses the phase. The preamble is reversals and the tail is steady carrier, so the other station can open and close DCD. Latin-1 characters above ASCII 127 are sent. Squelch and AFC are the same switches as RTTY. |
| QPSK | QPSK31, QPSK63, QPSK125, QPSK250, QPSK500 | K=5 convolutional code. One dibit per varicode bit. The phase blend covers the whole symbol. 8PSK is not on the menu. |
| Olivia | 4/250, 8/250, 8/500, 16/500, 16/1000, 32/1000 | Gray-coded tones. Baud 62.5, 31.25, 62.5, 31.25, 62.5, 31.25. |
| Contestia | the same six | Same shape as Olivia, Contestia scramble. |
| MFSK | MFSK4, 8, 11, 16, 22, 31, 32, 64, 64L, 128, 128L | NASA K=7, varicode, diagonal interleaver. The L rows use the long interleaver. |
| DominoEX | Micro, 4, 5, 8, 11, 16, 22 | 18-tone incremental keying. FEC is off. Micro is 2 baud. |
| Thor | 4, 5, 8, 11, 16, 22, 25x4, 50x1, 50x2, 100 | Slow speeds are NASA K=7. 25x4, 50x1, 50x2, and 100 are IEEE K=15. 18-tone incremental keying. |
| MT63 | 500S, 500L, 1000S, 1000L, 2000S, 2000L | 64 carriers, Walsh code, short or long interleave. Centered on the cursor. |
| JT65 | JT65A, JT65B, JT65C | MSHV generator. 60 s. Tone 0 stays near 1270 Hz. DIGU. |
| Q65 | Q65A, Q65B, Q65C, Q65D | MSHV generator. 30 s. The tone follows the cursor. DIGU. |
| MSK144 | MSK144, MSKMS | Meteor scatter, MSHV generator and decoder. 72 ms frames at 2000 baud, tones fixed at 1000 and 2000 Hz, on 500 to 2500 Hz around 1500 Hz, repeated from 250 ms into the slot until 370 ms before it ends. Pings print as they land, anywhere in the period. The MSK row above the waterfall holds MSHV's TX/RX period (5, 7.5, 10, 15, or 30 s, 30 s by default for both), DF Tol (10 to 400 Hz, 150 by default), Sh (MSK40 short messages), and SWL. Under it is MSHV's ping display: time across the period, frequency up, the period on the air on top and the one before under it, with a mark where each decode came from. The receive list uses MSHV's columns: Time, T, dB, DF, Message, Navg, Bit err, Eye, Freq. DIGU. Dials on 6 m and the 2 m chip: 50.260 and 144.150 MHz, Region 1 50.280 and 144.360 MHz. MSK144 sends FT8-style messages and contest exchanges, and MSK40 `<CALL1 CALL2> RPT` frames when that option is on. MSKMS is MSHV's variant with 72-bit messages and the duration-strength report (26, R27) from the ping you heard; Tx2 sends that report. |
| JS8 | one row, labeled JS8 | JS8Call-improved Normal. 15 s, 50 Hz, JS8 Costas. The screen is band activity, the conversation, and call activity, with HB, CQ, Reply, SNR?, INFO?, STATUS?, GRID?, Directed, and Deselect. Heartbeat, CQ, and directed frames go out at the next 15 s UTC period, one frame each, starting half a second in. A longer message continues on the following periods. Reply and Deselect do not key. Band buttons QSY to the suggested calling dials, 160 m through 6 m. 2 m is in that guide and is not on the row. Changing mode clears the receive and transmit panes. |
| RADE | RADE V1, RADE V2 (experimental) | FreeDV RADE digital voice from the FreeDV rade_c library. The mic is 16 kHz, decoded speech plays at 48 kHz, and the modem is 8 kHz. V1 is about 1500 Hz wide, V2 about 860 Hz, and they do not talk to each other. DIGL on 160, 80, and 40 m, DIGU on 60 m and 30 m and up, the FreeDV convention (60 m is the 5.3 chip after 3.5: 5.3685 and 5.4035 MHz, the FreeDV Reporter dials); both stations must be on the same sideband. TX waits about a quarter second to fill its buffer so the modem frames go out without gaps. The V1 end-of-over carries your callsign from Options → User, coded the way the FreeDV app codes it, so FreeDV stations and FreeDV Reporter show your call, and Heard on the RADE screen shows the call read from the other station's end-of-over. Offset shows how far the station you receive is from where it should be, in Hz, as the FreeDV app shows it: plus is above, minus below, and RADE tracks only about 50 Hz either way. The microphone is levelled before the encoder (toward -23 LUFS, at most +12 dB, with a -1 dBFS limiter), as FreeDV does. The radio audio is resampled with a band-limited filter in both directions. 200 ms of silence follows the end-of-over before TX drops. Drive 100 is the FreeDV app's level. Gain, -10 to 30 dB, sits after the microphone and before the speech-level bar. The bar shows peaks after the leveler, from 0 to 1, with a mark at half scale. The trace beside SNR is the last 3 minutes, from -10 to 35 dB. The top bar shows the radio dial in every mode: green while receiving, red while transmitting, gray when the radio is not connected, with DIGU or DIGL in the box. Mic NR runs RNNoise noise suppression on the microphone before the encoder, and BW expand runs the Opus bandwidth expander on decoded speech, filling in 8 to 24 kHz; both are on by default, as in the FreeDV app, and both are on the RADE audio row. FreeDV Reporter, under Options → Network Configuration, is Off until On is chosen; then STD connects to qso.freedv.org only while RADE is the mode, shows your call, locator, dial and TX, and reports each RADE station heard with its SNR (an empty call while in sync, as the FreeDV app does). The box beside On is the Message column text; the RADE screen has the same box as Reporter message. In every mode that spots, the bottom status strip has a chip for its reporter (PSK RPT, WSPRNET, or FREEDV RPT): click the name to open that service's page in the browser, and On/Off to turn it on or off. BPF is V2 only. RX and TX work like the radio's switch: RX is green while receiving, TX shows ON AIR while transmitting, and clicking RX (or TX again) ends the over with the end-of-over and your callsign, with RX amber for that moment. Halt is not shown in RADE, because it would drop without your callsign. Scan, after RX and TX, steps the FreeDV Reporter calling dials from 160 m to 10 m and listens for RADE on each. Dwell sets how long each dial is held, 5 to 30 seconds. With Stay on sync on, Scan stops on the first dial where a station syncs; with it off, Scan stays while the station is in sync and moves on a few seconds after it drops. Click Scan again to stop, or to start from the next dial. Right-click Scan to pick the bands: All bands, or any of 160, 80, 60, 40, 20, 17, 15, 12, and 10 m; the chip then reads Scan 40 m or Scan 3 bands. Dials you add with Extra for a band are scanned too (right-click Extra to edit, delete, add, or clear them), after that band's reporter dials. A band click, TX, or a mode change stops it. Scan only moves the dial. Leave radio compression and NR off and set Drive so ALC barely moves. Show columns places Audio, Status, and Waveform side by side, and Show rows stacks them. The reporter table is on the RADE screen: Filter, All or This band, a soft red tint while a station is transmitting and a soft green tint while it is receiving, double-click to tune, Request QSY, Ignore requests, Log contact, up to 15 saved messages, and a TX limit of 3 minutes. Gain runs from -10 to 30 dB. |
| WSPR | one row, labeled WSPR | WSPR-2 from WSJT-X and JTDX. Even UTC minutes. Band buttons QSY to the WSPR dials. Tx power, Tx Pct, Band Hopping. No macro bar. Locator is Options → User. Receive titles stay above the scrolling rows: Time, dB, DT, Frequency, Drift, Call, Locator, Report. A rule separates the periods. DT is the time offset. Report is the dBm in the message. Decodes stay in the receive colour after a red TX echo. Options → Network can post each heard station to WSPRnet. That post is off until WSPRnet is set to On, and those messages show only while WSPR is the mode. WSPRnet does not ask for a password. The reports page lists stations this callsign reported. |

Hell, RTTY, PSK, QPSK, Olivia, Contestia, MFSK, DominoEX, Thor, and MT63 show a QSO bar for the station being worked: Call, Name, QTH, St, Pr, Loc, RST in, Out, and Notes. Right-click a received word to fill a field. Double-click a callsign to fill Call. Name, QTH, the report they gave you, their locator, and their call are read from their text and offered faintly in the empty fields; a click or Tab takes one. A call already in STD's log, or in a second ADIF log set in Options → Lookup, fills the empty fields from the last QSO in blue and shows how many times and when you worked them. STD keeps a copy of that second log in its own log folder, so moving or deleting the original does not lose those stations; Options → Log Options → Open log folder shows it. HamQTH or QRZ.com, also in Options → Lookup, fills what is still empty in green; a portable call (with a slash) gets only the name. Type over any of them; what is in the box is logged. Auto RST fills Out from the TCI S-meter and stays off until you turn it on. Log writes ADIF. JS8, WSPR, WEFAX, and the FT8 family do not show the bar. The Feld Hell tape is a draggable pane over the RX log, CLR RX clears the tape, CLR SENT clears the sent text, and Save tape writes a PNG under Documents\STD\hell. On those keyboard modes a finished pass takes the sent text off the front of the transmit box, as Fldigi does, so the next TX sends only new text. A stopped pass takes the characters that already went out. Text changed during the pass is left alone. JS8 keeps its own queue. RTTY, PSK, and QPSK send live, as Fldigi does: TX keys and sends what is in the box, text typed while on the air goes out as it is typed, and an empty box sends idle (LTRS in RTTY, phase reversals in PSK) without dropping TRX. Press TX once to end after the box is sent, and again to stop at once; characters not yet on the air go back into the box. A ▶ macro (one with `<TX>`) keys when Macros key is on and, with `<RX>`, returns to RX when its text is done. A macro pressed while on the air is added to the box. Back to RX after (on, 25 s by default; 5, 10, 15, or 25 s) ends a live transmission by itself when nothing new was typed for that long; turn it off to stay keyed until you press TX.

Options → Speech… reads received text aloud with a Windows voice on a sound device you pick, not through the radio. It reads RTTY, PSK, QPSK, Olivia, Contestia, MFSK, DominoEX, Thor, and MT63 a line at a time, or a run of finished words on a long line, and waits quietly for new text. Callsigns and reports are spelled out, noise runs can be skipped, and it stops while you transmit. Hell is a picture and is not read; nor are JS8 or the FT8 family. With read-back on, a Speech chip beside CLR RX mutes and unmutes, and the number beside it is the volume. It is off until you turn it on. Talk does the reverse: it turns your voice into transmit text. In Options → Speech… → Transmit pick a microphone, set the gate, and download a speech model (75 to 490 MB, fetched only when you choose one, so STD itself stays small). Then hold Talk and speak. The words land at the end of the transmit box with callsigns and reports written the ham way (Kilo Eight Alpha Bravo becomes K8AB, five nine nine becomes 599), and nothing keys until you press TX. **My words** in the same tab lists what Talk should expect (club calls, names, towns, your rig and antenna); your name and QTH from Options → User and the station in the QSO bar are added by themselves. **Corrections** fixes words it keeps getting wrong, one per line as `heard = send`. To teach one on the spot, select the wrong text in the transmit box, right-click, and pick Talk: always send … as, or add it to My words.

TX or Tune is what sends `trx`. An empty line does not key.

## Receive only

**WEFAX-576.** IOC 576, 120 lines per minute. Switching to it hides the Hell box, the TX buttons, and the macros. Black is 400 Hz below the cursor and white is 400 Hz above it, about 1 kHz wide around the 1900 Hz marker. A 2.4 kHz USB filter passes it. Reception mixes to the cursor, filters, then reads the FM. The stations pane lists assigned frequencies by ITU region. Auto uses the locator in Options → User. NOAA hours are marked on the frequencies that have them. A click sets the dial 1.9 kHz under the assigned frequency, USB, marker 1900 Hz, and does not key. A 300 Hz start tone opens a picture and a 450 Hz stop tone saves it. Start opens a chart that is already running, aligned to the top under the toolbar. A click on the picture sets the left edge, and a right-click puts it back. Slant, next to Save, is parts per million, 10 ppm a step. Zero is 120 lines per minute. Fit sizes the chart to the pane. Auto save, Open folder, and Save write PNGs. TX does not send this mode, and START is never sent.

## PSK Reporter

Options → Network Configuration. Enable PSK Reporter style spots is off until checked. Antenna is the description sent with each spot. Also send WSPR spots to PSK Reporter is a separate checkbox and stays off. The map uses the callsign already saved under Options → User. There is no second callsign or locator. It does not send START, and it does not key.

## WSPRnet

Options → Network Configuration. Off and On. On posts each WSPR station STD decodes. Off posts nothing. WSPRnet does not ask for a password. It does not post your own transmission. Options → WSPRnet reports opens the page of stations reported by the callsign in Options → User. There is no second callsign or locator. It does not send START, and it does not key.

## File

File → Backup settings saves layouts and the callsign and locator already stored under Options → User. It does not copy the TCI host or port. File → Import settings restores that file and leaves the TCI host and port as they are. File → Backup logbook copies the ADIF file and leaves the original in place. File → Export ADIF writes a copy another logger can import. None of this keys, and START is never sent.

## Halt

Halt ends the transmission immediately, even in the middle of a slot, turns TRX off, and stops the sequence. If nothing is transmitting, Halt does not key. Tx1 through Tx6 stay as they are.

## Decode

Options → Decode sets Fast, Normal, or Deep, and AP. Fast, Normal, Deep, and AP reach Q65. AP reaches JT65. Decode → JT65 and Q65 holds averaging, auto clear after a Q65 decode, Clear Averaging Now, Q65 single signal, EME delay, drift correction, JT65 VHF/UHF, deep search against the ADIF log, and the JT65 signal cap. Averages clear on a mode change or a dial move of more than 1 kHz. None of this keys.

## Waterfall

Floor is the palette bottom in dB. Range is how many dB the palette spans. A louder peak stretches that span, so the skirts of a strong trace stay colored. Auto sets the floor under the noise. Flatten levels the band slope. The picture updates once per turn, so a quiet band does not keep the CPU and the GPU busy. Options → Graphics Auto uses Direct3D 11 on Windows and Vulkan elsewhere, then Painter if that device fails. Vulkan is an explicit Graphics choice and restarts STD. Hell, RTTY, PSK, QPSK, Olivia, Contestia, MFSK, DominoEX, Thor, MT63, WSPR, and WEFAX center the marker on the carrier. FT8, FT4, FT2, JT65, Q65, and JS8 start at the cursor. Speed, Start, and BW on the mode bar keep their last character. The Extra caption is the word Extra. View → Save layout stores Floor, Range, and Auto with that mode. None of this keys.

## Not on the menu

These do not key.

- **8PSK.** A different constellation from QPSK. Not on the menu.
- **Thor Micro.** Not in this build.
- MSHV modes still outside this program stay listed in [mshv-modes.md](mshv-modes.md): JTMS, FSK441, FSK315, ISCAT-A, ISCAT-B, JT6M, and PI4. They are leftover and not shipping. The 11025 Hz family cannot run on TCI (no 44100). JT65, Q65, MSK144, and MSKMS are on the menu.

CW stays in Lyra.

## Later

FLD-88 is queued. It is not in this build. JS8 Fast, Turbo, Slow, and Ultra are a different symbol clock and are not on the menu.
